Item three

Spacing — recorded, not disputed

In several beds the called-for quantity does not fit the called-for area at the on-center spacing the plan specifies. I raised this on site and asked for direction. Peter Kaufman, the landscape architect, directed that they be set as tight as needed to get the whole count inside the selected area without increasing the footprint.

That is what we did and I am not looking to revisit it. The result is that planted density in those beds is tighter than the on-center spacing stated on the sheet.

Recording it for one reason: the beds will look full and correct at handover, but material planted below its specified spacing crowds as it matures — grasses close in the center, shrubs grow into each other and thin out underneath. If that shows up during the warranty period, this is the reason, and the decision is documented here rather than reconstructed later.

If anyone wants the spacing opened back up in specific beds, tell me which and I will adjust. Otherwise we proceed as directed.

Scheduling

What is outstanding is material, not labor

Every bed that is planted but short has its open spots flagged on site. The layout is done and the holes are located, so when material arrives it goes straight in the ground — no re-staking, no re-measuring, no second trip to work out placement.

Beds affected by item one are being left flagged as incomplete rather than closed out, so nothing reads as finished that is not.
